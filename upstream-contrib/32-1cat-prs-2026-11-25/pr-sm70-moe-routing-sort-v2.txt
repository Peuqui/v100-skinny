Problem: Nvfp4SkinnySm70Experts groups the routing slots of a decode or verify step by expert in one small Triton program (group_routing). The kernel of #1286 ranks the slots pairwise (an S x S comparison in registers, four warps): its cost grows with the square of the slot count, 2.2 us for one token but about 14 us at five tokens (40 slots) and 83 us at 16 tokens (128 slots) on a V100, per MoE layer and per call. GLM-5.3-Flash has 42 MoE layers in a round.

Change:
- vllm/model_executor/layers/fused_moe/sm70/routing.py: the kernel builds one sort key per slot, expert * BLOCK + slot (the keys are distinct, so sorting them is the stable sort by expert; the slot index rides in the low bits), runs one tl.sort, finds the group starts by comparing neighbours (tl.gather) and a cumsum, and scatters the starts into the group rows (the unused rows are written first, a debug barrier orders the two writes). Padding lanes take the largest key. The program is one warp; for at most 128 keys more warps only add shared-memory round trips. group_routing returns the same int32 tensors perm, gids and goff as before and uses group_routing_reference (the torch version that defines the result) above 128 slots, as before.
- tests/kernels/moe/test_sm70_group_routing_cpu.py (new): the kernel against group_routing_reference in the Triton interpreter on the CPU. It skips itself unless TRITON_INTERPRET=1 is set before Triton is imported.

Evidence:
- Kernel time in a CUDA graph (200 launches per graph, GLM-5.3-Flash shape 288 experts top 8, the pairwise kernel of #1286 (a verbatim copy of it in the benchmark script, which is not part of this PR) against the sort kernel with one warp; microseconds per call, lower is better; every row checked equal to the torch reference before timing): Tesla V100, 8 / 40 / 48 / 64 / 128 slots: 2.15 -> 1.67, 13.96 -> 2.24, 12.73 -> 2.02, 12.74 -> 2.01, 83.36 -> 2.33. Quadro RTX 8000: 2.56 -> 2.05, 17.78 -> 2.61, 15.55 -> 2.28, 14.45 -> 2.11, 58.06 -> 2.28. The DeepSeek-V4-Flash shape (256 experts top 6) shows the same: 48 slots 12.43 -> 1.98 (V100), 13.65 -> 1.99 (RTX 8000), 96 slots 83.37 -> 2.33 and 58.10 -> 2.27. The number of warps: on the V100, two and four warps were no faster than one by more than 0.02 us at any measured slot count and slower from 40 slots up (40 slots: 2.38 and 2.40 against 2.24 us). On the Quadro RTX 8000, two and four warps were faster for the DeepSeek-V4-Flash shape at 6 slots (1.58 and 1.58 against 1.66 us) and for the GLM-5.3-Flash shape at 40 slots (2.44 and 2.45 against 2.61 us), equal within 0.03 us at GLM 8 and 48 slots and DeepSeek 12 and 24 slots, and slower at GLM 64 and 128 slots and DeepSeek 48 and 96 slots (GLM 128 slots: 2.41 and 2.52 against 2.28 us; DeepSeek 96 slots: 2.41 and 2.50 against 2.27 us); the kernel keeps one warp, which leaves about 0.17 us per call at the five-token GLM round (40 slots) on the RTX 8000 unused. The benchmark ran on 2026-11-23 on a branch of our fork whose routing.py is byte-identical to this branch's, with the stock Triton 3.6.0 of the environment (the kernel uses no tl.dot, so the sm7x Triton of #1282 is not needed for it).
- Per round, computed from these micro-benchmark figures and the layer counts of a GLM-5.3-Flash profile (21 MoE layers on the V100 stages, 21 on the RTX stages), not measured end to end: at five tokens the sort kernel saves (13.96 - 2.24) us x 21 + (17.78 - 2.61) us x 21 = 0.56 ms per decode round, of a round of 55 to 57 ms in our pipeline setup (3x V100 + 2x Quadro RTX 8000, k = 4).
- Outputs are bit-identical to the torch reference: tests/kernels/moe/test_sm70_group_routing.py (CUDA, in #1286) and the interpreter test compare perm, gids and goff with torch.equal for random routings (CUDA test: 9, 64 and 288 experts with up to 8 per token and 1 to 16 tokens; interpreter test: GLM-5.3-Flash 288 experts top 8, DeepSeek-V4-Flash 256 experts top 6 and a 9-expert shape), all slots on one expert, all slots on distinct experts, int64 ids and a slot count off the power of two; the interpreter test adds heavy ties with one to five experts, 128 slots, and expert ids at the ends of the supported range (up to 16,000,000, and negative ids); both files include a 320-slot case that takes the torch reference.
- There is no end-to-end measurement of this change alone, and none is claimed.
- Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash and DeepSeek-V4-Flash shapes, V100 and Quadro RTX 8000, up to 128 slots.
