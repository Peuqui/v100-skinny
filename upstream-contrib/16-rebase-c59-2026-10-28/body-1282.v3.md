## Purpose

Stock Triton lowers every tl.dot to FMA before SM80, so the SM70-family routes run cuBLAS or hand-written kernels on Volta and Turing. I built a Triton 3.6 with the sm_70 (mma.m8n8k4, through linear layouts) and sm_75 (mma.m16n8k8) lowerings: github.com/Peuqui/triton, branch sm7x-tensor-cores (23 commits on v3.6.0), shown upstream in https://github.com/triton-lang/triton/discussions/12517. With it, Triton kernels on tensor cores beat several of the DeepSeek-V4 prefill routes on both card types. This PR adds one opt-in setting for such a Triton and the routes that use it. With the setting on and a stock Triton the engine stops when the first layer that uses the setting is built, and says why, instead of running FMA kernels; models without such a layer are unaffected.

Stacked PR. The branch sits on #1280 (DeepSeek-V4 prefill, itself on #1279, which stacks on #1278 and #1216; #1222 and #1227 from that stack are merged). The copies of those PRs are identical to the open ones, except that the copy of #1216 differs from it in the anchors of the generated docs/design/architecture/runtime_reference.md. Only the last nine commits are new here, so please review those (git log 32e4372db..bff037870, where 32e4372db is the head of #1280).

The nine commits:

1. The setting (e9dbf58e5): kernel_config.triton_sm7x_tensor_cores (default false, part of the compilation hash). KernelConfig.use_triton_sm7x_tensor_cores(device_index=None) is the one place routes ask. It answers for the device's own architecture and, on first use per architecture, compiles a 16x16 fp16 tl.dot without launching it and requires mma.sync in the PTX (vllm/triton_utils/mma_probe.py, generic).
2. DeepSeek-V4 indexer prefill scores in one Triton kernel (e2ad1f3ad): heads of BLOCK_T tokens stacked into one MMA operand that every key tile reuses, e4m3 keys widened bit-exactly to fp16, products rounded to fp16 as the cuBLAS route stores them, relu, head weights and head sum in registers. It replaces the cuBLAS route on Volta (28-39 % faster summed over 4.8k to 76.8k keys) and serves Turing chunks with head counts other than 32 and 64. Turing keeps the fused native scorer of #1280 for 32 and 64 heads, since the Triton kernel is 1.0-4.7 % slower there summed over long contexts.
3. Skinny MoE top-k reduction in one pass (a5597e08b): the batched _mtp_weighted_reduce kernel the FP4 stages already use (bit-identical to moe_unpermute) replaces a five-kernel Torch chain. This commit does not depend on the setting: it applies to every skinny QPN MoE (DeepSeek-V4, GLM-5.3 and the other NVFP4 and MXFP4 checkpoints on this path), and 0.02-0.04 % of their outputs change by one fp16 step (one FMA rounding instead of a product and a sum rounding; the error against fp64 is equal or lower).
4. mHC pre-stage projection on tensor cores for prefill (7a1c01d83): the fp32 weight is split into fp16 hi + lo * 2^-11, which keeps 22 of 24 mantissa bits; every 16-wide K block starts from zero, block sums add round-to-nearest in two levels, and 32 K splits are summed in fp64. Against fp64 the maximum error over 20 inputs is lower than the current route on both cards (lower on 20 of 20 inputs on the RTX 8000, on 16 of 20 on the V100). Decode (<= 16 tokens) keeps its kernels.
5. DeepSeek-V4 sparse MLA prefill matmuls that read the selected rows through the indices (fd303bbcd), instead of gathering a [T, W, 512] workspace for two cuBLAS BMMs. Bit-identical on the V100; on the RTX 8000 0.97-1.25 % of the fp16 outputs differ by one step, with the error against fp64 equal.
6. Volta skinny MoE on a Triton grouped GEMM for long chunks (ceef5239d, 919403d60, c51b1d40b): with more than 13 rows per expert on average a grouped GEMM that decodes each FP4 tile once per row block beats the hand kernel, which decodes it per 8-row block. Programs map to row blocks of one group in three row tiles (16, 32 and 64 rows) and find their block inside the kernel. The threshold was measured again on the final kernel (Test Result). The route needs commit 3: without its FP32 copies of y2 the skinny chunk bound is 682 tokens at DeepSeek-V4's shapes, which average at most 16 rows per expert; commit 3 raises it to 1365. Turing keeps its m16n8k8 hand kernel.
7. One sparse prefill pass per chunk (0356875b6): the route of commit 5 attends up to 1024 query tokens per pass on every card instead of 128. Its buffers grow from 30 to 240 MiB per pass at width 640 and from 54 to 432 MiB at width 1152; the gather route keeps 128-token passes.
