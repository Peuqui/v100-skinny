Problem: MLAAttention multiplies by the FP16 kv_b weight twice per layer in a decode or speculative-verify step: q_nope times W_UK_T (the absorption) and attn_out times W_UV (the value up-projection), 64 heads of 256 x 512, 16.8 MB each, as two torch.bmm calls. With at most eight rows every call is bound by reading that weight, and in an nsys profile of GLM-5.3-Flash decode (verify rounds of five rows, that is four drafted tokens, pipeline of V100 and Quadro RTX 8000 stages) the cuBLAS kernels streamed it at 40 to 54 % of the cards' peak bandwidth (V100 45.8 and 40.4 %, RTX 8000 53.7 and 53.7 %). GLM-5.3-Flash has five such layers on the V100 stages and six on the RTX stages per round.

Change:
- vllm/model_executor/layers/attention/mla_kv_b_skinny.py (new): a Triton kernel with one program per head and BLOCK_N output columns. It reads its weight tile once for all rows (at most eight, one 16-row MMA tile with the unused rows masked) and runs tl.dot with FP32 accumulation and a single rounding to FP16 at the end, like the cuBLAS kernels it replaces; only the summation order differs, so results are not bit-identical to cuBLAS. Tiles per compute capability (70 and 75) come from a sweep on GLM-5.3-Flash's shapes (benchmark --sweep over 1, 5 and 7 rows): for each weight and card the tile with the smallest summed time over the three row counts (for W_UV on the Quadro RTX 8000 two tiles tie at 96.11 us; the table keeps the one with two warps). A weight that is not FP16, has no contiguous axis or is not divisible by the tile raises at load time.
- vllm/model_executor/layers/attention/mla_attention.py: MLAAttention decides once, in process_weights_after_loading, whether the layer takes the kernel (kernel_config.use_mla_kv_b_skinny for the weight's device) and keeps the two tiles; _kv_b_bmm sends products of two to eight rows to the kernel (KV_B_SKINNY_MIN_ROWS = 2, KV_B_SKINNY_MAX_ROWS = 8) and everything else, a single row included, to torch.bmm as before.
- vllm/config/kernel.py: new field mla_kv_b_skinny, default False (so nothing changes unless it is set), ignored in KernelConfig.compute_hash (the kernel runs inside the opaque attention op, outside the compiled graph). Setting it without triton_sm7x_tensor_cores raises. On a device whose compute capability major version is not 7 it does nothing. On a device of major version 7 other than 70 and 75 (72 or 73, for example) the layer raises when its weights are loaded, because the tile table has entries for 70 and 75 only (kv_b_skinny_tile raises ValueError; the sm7x probe may stop it first); I did not run this on such a device, and the tests cover capability 80 only. docs/design/architecture/runtime_reference.md is regenerated for the new field.
- benchmarks/kernels/benchmark_mla_kv_b_skinny.py: cuBLAS against the kernel in a CUDA graph, with a tile sweep. tests/kernels/test_mla_kv_b_skinny.py: configuration, dispatch and numerics (see Tests).

Evidence:
- Micro-benchmark, five rows, one product per call in a CUDA graph, 8 weight copies cycled so every call streams from DRAM, microseconds per call (lower is better), cuBLAS against the kernel with the tile table of this branch: Tesla V100 W_UK_T 34.12 -> 26.12, W_UV 34.34 -> 24.21; Quadro RTX 8000 W_UK_T 41.31 -> 31.90, W_UV 40.44 -> 32.05. The tiles are the sweep winners by summed time over 1, 5 and 7 rows, and they are also the fastest at five rows. With these tiles the maximum absolute difference to cuBLAS is 0 at five and at seven rows on both cards; at one row it is 0.0312 (W_UK_T) and 0.0625 (W_UV) on both cards.
- Seven rows, the largest verify round of the end-to-end runs below (six drafted tokens), same method and tiles, cuBLAS -> kernel: Tesla V100 W_UK_T 34.69 -> 26.85, W_UV 35.02 -> 24.77; Quadro RTX 8000 W_UK_T 41.90 -> 32.39, W_UV 40.96 -> 32.96.
- At one row the kernel is not faster on the V100 and gains under 2 % on the Quadro RTX 8000. With the table's tiles at one row: Tesla V100 W_UK_T 24.52 us against 23.79 for cuBLAS, W_UV 25.47 against 23.82 (3 and 7 % slower); Quadro RTX 8000 W_UK_T 30.90 against 31.46, W_UV 31.10 against 31.60 (1.8 and 1.6 % faster). The dispatch therefore keeps one-row products (plain decode without speculation) on torch.bmm and takes the kernel from two rows on. cuBLAS needs 8 to 10 us more at two rows than at one (V100 33.46 and 33.78 us against 23.79 and 23.82; Quadro RTX 8000 40.93 and 39.84 against 31.46 and 31.60). I did not sweep two, three, four, six or eight rows with the table's tiles; with the benchmark's default tiles (not the table's) the W_UK_T kernel is faster than cuBLAS at two rows on both cards (V100 26.03 against 33.46 us, RTX 8000 31.53 against 40.93), while the default W_UV tile ties with cuBLAS on the V100 (33.83 against 33.78) and is faster on the RTX 8000 (31.84 against 39.84).
- End to end, GLM-5.3-Flash NVFP4 with the DFlash2 FP8 drafter, pipeline parallel 5 over 3x V100 + 2x Quadro RTX 8000, one request at a time, num_speculative_tokens 6 with CUDA graph capture size 7 and adaptive verify length on in all arms (the verify length varies from one to six drafted tokens per round, so a round has two to seven rows; first round four drafted tokens), measured on our fork at fork commit 48a92aa11 (before the port to this branch; the kernel module and its test file are byte-identical to this branch's before the later change that keeps single-row products on torch.bmm; the rounds measured here have two or more rows, so that change does not matter for them), arms T P T P with the setting on in T: greedy round 53.4 and 53.5 ms against 53.7 and 54.1 ms (lower is better); sampling with temperature and min_p, all texts, 45.78 and 45.40 tok/s against 45.54 and 45.19 tok/s (+0.5 %); GSM8K decode 70.98 and 70.87 tok/s against 70.74 and 70.37 tok/s (+0.5 %), 29 of 30 correct in every arm. T is the fork production tree of that day plus two host-side cleanup commits of ours plus this commit; the cleanup commits alone gave sampling 45.65 and 45.66 tok/s against 45.57 and 45.60 (+0.15 %) in their own A/B on GLM (greedy round 53.6 and 53.6 ms against 53.7 and 53.6 ms), so part of the half percent is theirs. The gain at the model level is therefore about half a percent at most. It is small because the micro-benchmark saves about 18 us per layer at five rows (8.0 + 10.1 us on a V100, 9.4 + 8.4 us on a Quadro RTX 8000; at seven rows 7.8 + 10.3 and 9.5 + 8.0 us), about 0.2 ms over the 11 layers of a round, against a round of about 54 ms; the measured round difference is 0.45 ms (mean 53.45 against 53.9 ms).

- Scope: measured only on GLM-5.3-Flash, V100 and Quadro RTX 8000; row counts 1 to 8 in the micro-benchmark with its default tiles and 1, 5 and 7 with the table's tiles (the numerical tests cover every row count from 1 to 8 with the table's tiles); the end-to-end rounds have two to seven rows.
