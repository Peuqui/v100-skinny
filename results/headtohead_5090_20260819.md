# Head-to-head: 4× V100 + v100-skinny vs 1× RTX 5090 + ninfer (2026-08-19)

Both engines run **in our lab, by us, through one harness**. No published
figures are used in the comparison; theirs are quoted only as a reproduction
check.

## Configuration (from each engine's own receipts)

| | ours | ninfer |
|---|---|---|
| hardware | 4× Tesla V100-SXM2-16GB, TP4 | 1× RTX 5090 (sm_120), CUDA 13.1 |
| engine | 1Cat-vLLM 1.2.2 + QPN kernels, MT=2 on | ninfer built from source @ `a99407c6` |
| weights | RadixArk Qwen3.8-27B NVFP4 (verbatim) | `qwen3_8_27b_nvfp4.ninfer` (unsloth NVFP4), sha256 verified |
| KV cache | fp16 | bf16 (`engine.kv_cache: bf16`) |
| concurrency | `MNS=1` | `max_concurrency: 1` |
| speculation | MTP k=7 | `speculative_backend: mtp`, `draft_window: 3` |
| prefix reuse | enabled (1Cat SM70 default) | `prefix_reuse: False` |

ninfer's `--request-log-jsonl` supplies `speculative.{accepted_tokens,
drafted_tokens,rounds}` per request, so **acceptance and tokens/round are
measured on both sides** rather than reported for ours only.

## Reproduction check — our ninfer run vs their published numbers

AIME f01, five seeds, their sampling profile:

| | ours-run ninfer | their published |
|---|---:|---:|
| completion tokens | 1,463.6 ± 237.7 | 1,465.4 ± 417.3 |
| acceptance | 76.5% ± 2.4 | 76.0% ± 2.4 |
| tokens/round | 3.30 ± 0.07 | 3.28 ± 0.07 |
| decode tok/s | 189.5 ± 4.3 | 195.2 ± 4.6 |

Completion length reproduces to **0.1%**, acceptance to 0.5 pp, tokens/round
to 0.02. Rate lands 2.9% under theirs. The harness is measuring the same
thing they measured.

## AIME 2026 f01 — five seeds each, both engines answer 277 on every seed

| | ours (medium) | ours (xhigh) | ninfer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| decode tok/s | **206.8 ± 4.0** | 169.4 ± 3.5 | 189.5 ± 4.3 |
| completion tokens | 1,514 ± 44 | 1,212 ± 110 | 1,463.6 ± 237.7 |
| tokens/round | **5.93** | 4.86 | 3.30 |
| round latency | 28.7 ms | — | **17.4 ms** |
| correct | 5/5 | 5/5 | 5/5 |

**1.09× at the length-matched arm.** Qwen3.8's template defaults
`reasoning_effort` to `xhigh`, which injects a "think carefully…" system
message; `medium` injects nothing. ninfer runs no Jinja template and rejects
`chat_template_kwargs` entirely, so it injects no such instruction — and its
measured completion length (1,463.6) sits 3.4% from our `medium` (1,514) and
17% from our `xhigh` (1,212). Both arms are reported; `medium` is the
like-for-like one.

## The mechanism, measured on both sides

**Their round is 17.34–17.48 ms on every cell, and 17.4 ms on AIME. Flat.
Ours is 24.94–25.36 ms.** We are ~44% slower per round and win anyway,
wherever acceptance converts the deficit.

The sharpest instance is doc2json: **ninfer's acceptance is 100.0%, which
pins it at exactly 4.00 tokens/round — the hard ceiling of MTP3.** It cannot
go faster there without more draft depth. We take 7.83. That is the
m8n8k4-tile argument made visible: verifying 8 rows costs 1.05–1.16× verifying
one, so k=7 is close to free on our verify path.

It also cuts the other way, honestly: at 100% acceptance **more depth would
obviously help them too**. What it costs them is now directly measurable via
`--draft-tokens` and should be measured rather than asserted.

## Caveats

- **Prefix caching is mismatched** — enabled on ours (1Cat SM70 MTP default),
  `--no-prefix-reuse` on theirs. Each cell runs once with a distinct prompt so
  the effect should be near zero, but it is an unmatched control.
- **Domain cells are n=1**; AIME is n=5. Ratios near unity (mergesort 1.08,
  math 1.00, code 0.96) sit inside plausible timing jitter.
- **Different quantisation producers** — unsloth vs RadixArk. Same *recipe*
  structure (NVFP4 on `mlp.{gate,up,down}_proj`, FP8 elsewhere), different
  weights. Converting ours needs the ~54 GB BF16 base, which does not fit on
  the 5090's disk.
- **Context differs between our two runs**: domain cells at MML 16384 (matched
  to ninfer), AIME at 98304. MML is not timing-neutral for us — 16384 gives a
  ~25.1 ms round against 26.7 at 32768 — so these figures are not
  interchangeable with the committed v1.1 product table.
- This is a **system** comparison: engine, hardware generation and
  quantisation producer all differ. No claim about kernel quality follows.

## Method notes worth keeping

- ninfer streams thinking as `reasoning_content`; vLLM uses `reasoning`.
  Reading only the latter puts `t_first` after the entire reasoning phase and
  inflates their rate badly.
- ninfer **rejects `chat_template_kwargs`** and takes thinking from the
  server-side `--no-thinking` flag, so a mixed-mode suite needs two boots.
- **Never leave sampling to defaults on either side.** ninfer fills unset
  fields from the artifact's model card — measured `presence_penalty 1.5,
  top_p 0.8, top_k 20`, not the CLI defaults its `--help` documents. At
  temperature 0 it does exact argmax and ignores them (verified: byte-identical
  output across a 1.5→0.0 presence swing), but at 0.6 they are live.

# UPDATE, same day — three corrections

Later work on 2026-08-19 changes how this document should be read. The
measurements above stand; two of the conclusions do not.

## 1. ninfer was benchmarked detuned

Their published `--draft-tokens 3` is not their optimum. Sweeping depth on
our own 5090 (`depth_cost_20260819.md`):

| cell | tok/s @3 | tok/s @5 | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| mergesort | 210.7 | 253.5 | +20.3% |
| json | 216.6 | 269.4 | +24.4% |
| log2json | 228.9 | 297.8 | +30.1% |
| prose | 136.3 | 124.2 | **-8.9%** |

At depth 5 they beat us on mergesort (253.5 vs 227.6) and json (269.4 vs
269.0) and draw level on log2json. **The "4 of 7 cells to us" headline was
measured against a detuned ninfer and does not survive.**

Their engine hard-caps at 5: `--spec mtp requires --draft-tokens in [1,5]`.

## 2. There is no single "best vs best"

Depth cost is constant (~1.32 ms per draft token, `round ~= 13.28 + 1.32k`)
and workload-independent; depth *benefit* is proportional to acceptance. So
the optimal depth is a property of the workload, not the engine — 3 on prose,
5 on structured cells. Any single-number "their best" is a choice, not a
measurement. Report strict / matched-depth / best-vs-best separately, and
name the depth in every cell.

## 3. Our AIME number was measured under the MML tax

This document's AIME arm booted at MML 98,304, which sits on the slow plateau
of `mml_ladder_20260819.md`. Pinning `VLLM_FLASH_V100_DECODE_PARTITION_SIZE=256`
recovers the fast plateau at unchanged capability
(`partition_fix_20260819.md`). **206.8 tok/s and the 1.09x ratio are
therefore floors**, and are superseded by the re-run recorded in
`aime_partfix_*`.

## Also unresolved

The checkpoints are still not matched: we serve RadixArk, their artifact is
built from `unsloth/Qwen3.8-27B-NVFP4 @ 60e813d4`. That revision **no longer
exists** — HF super-squashed the repo on 2026-08-15 and ModelScope carries
only `master` — so it cannot be reproduced exactly. Serving unsloth on our
stack is also a port, not a download: QPN8 is wired only into the ModelOpt
path, and unsloth ships its FP8 groups as compressed-tensors
`float-quantized`, which has no SM70 hardware path. Unsloth also puts FP8 on
more layers than RadixArk (`self_attn.(q|k|v|o)_proj`, layers 56-63 MLP, and
`lm_head`), so the swap is not timing-neutral either.
